01·THE PROBLEM

Space missions run on trade-offs

Everything competes

Oxygen, food, power, radiation and science all draw on the same limited resources.

Kids learn by doing

Trade-offs stick when a child feels their consequences, not when they memorise facts.

Most games oversimplify

Many learning games either ignore real constraints or leave out the science.

02·THE GAME

A Mars base, run by a kid

Free, in the browser

No download or install needed. Built for players aged 7 to 12.

Gale Crater base

The player runs a small research base in Gale Crater on Mars.

14 sols to succeed

Survive 14 sols, the Martian days, and send 32 rock samples back to Earth.

03·THE CHOICE

Ten energy blocks. Five systems.

Keep the crew alive

Life Support supplies air, and the Greenhouse grows the food the crew needs.

Protect or explore

The Storm Shield blocks solar-storm radiation, while the Rover collects rock samples.

Know what's coming

The Weather Station warns about solar storms one sol early, before Earth's warning arrives.

04·REAL NASA DATA

The rules come from real missions

up to 2.8×

Radiation peak during the 2017 solar storm (2.3× daily average), measured by Curiosity's RAD instrument.

½

A dust storm like Opportunity's 2018 storm halves solar power for that sol in the game.

+1

Each unit of rover power brings back one rock sample, inspired by Perseverance's 43 sample tubes.

05·LEARNING

Commander Nova explains. Kids decide.

Plain-language briefings

Commander Nova, powered by Google Gemini, turns game data into simple morning briefings.

Choices with consequences

Trade shield power for rover time, then see exactly what happens next.

Real science cards

After each sol, a short NASA fact about Mars or the biology of living in space.
